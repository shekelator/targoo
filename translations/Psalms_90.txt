A prayer of Moses, the man of God.

Lord, you have been our refuge in every generation.
Before the mountains were born, before you brought forth the earth's expanse —
from everlasting to everlasting, you are God.
You turn man back to dust and say, "Return, O children of Adam."
For a thousand years in your sight are like yesterday when it passes,
or like a watch in the night.
You sweep them away; they become like sleep —
in the morning they are like grass that sprouts anew.
In the morning it blossoms and sprouts; by evening it fades and withers.
For we are consumed by your anger and terrified by your wrath.
You have set our iniquities before you, our secret sins in the light of your face.
All our days pass away under your wrath; we spend our years like a sigh.
The days of our years — seventy years, or eighty if we are strong —
yet their span is only toil and trouble; they pass quickly, and we fly away.
Who knows the power of your anger? Your wrath matches the fear you are owed.
Teach us to number our days, that we may gain a heart of wisdom.
Relent, O LORD — how long before you have compassion on your servants?
Satisfy us in the morning with your steadfast love,
that we may sing for joy and be glad all our days.
Make us glad for as many days as you have afflicted us,
for as many years as we have seen trouble.
Let your works be seen by your servants, and your majesty by their children.
May the favor of the Lord our God rest upon us.
Establish the work of our hands — yes, may he establish the work of our hands!
